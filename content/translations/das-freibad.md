---
title: The Open-Air Pool
---

The open-air pool lies at the edge of town, between a sports ground and a strip of woodland, and has been in operation since the fifties. The basins are made of concrete, the changing rooms of wood, and the clock above the entrance has been two minutes fast for years. Anyone who comes at seven in the morning swims alone.

Three years ago the paper reported that the pool was costing the town two hundred and fifty thousand euros a year and was to be closed. The sums were not wrong: an open-air pool that is open on perhaps sixty days a year can hardly be defended in business terms. Had it been up to the town treasurer, the site would have been sold long ago.

That it turned out otherwise is down to an association founded by a retired teacher and a baker. They took over the kiosk, the lawn and part of the supervision; the town still pays for the water treatment, which nobody without a qualification is allowed to be responsible for. By now eighty volunteers work there, most of whom learnt to swim here themselves.

Whether that is a model for other municipalities is judged in different ways. Critics object that a public duty ought not to depend on whether enough volunteers can be found. The chairman does not even disagree. He only says that there are two possibilities: a pool that demands too much of the association, or none at all. As long as nothing third presents itself between the two, he says, he knows which he would choose.

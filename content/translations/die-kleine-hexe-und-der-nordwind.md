---
title: The Little Witch and the North Wind
---

The little witch Lila lives in a yellow house beside a large forest. She has a black hat, a brown broom and a book with many magic spells. But Lila cannot do magic very well yet. Often the soup turns blue, or her shoes dance through the kitchen. Then Lila has to laugh.

There has been no wind for three days. The leaves on the trees do not move. The washing hangs heavily on the line, and the windmill in the village stands still. Lila's broom cannot fly without wind either. It lies sadly outside the door and does not make a single little jump.

Lila goes into the forest and looks for the wind. She asks the birds, but they do not know the way. She asks an old fox, but he does not know anything either. Deep in the forest Lila suddenly hears a quiet whistling sound. It comes from a hollow tree beside a large grey rock.

A small forest troll is sitting behind the rock. He has green ears, a long red coat and a hat that is much too big. He is holding a blue bottle in his hands. White mist turns inside it. The bottle whistles, jumps and trembles, but a thick cork keeps it tightly closed.

Lila asks if the wind is in his bottle. The troll presses the bottle to his chest. His name is Knurz, and he is very angry with the north wind. Every morning the wind takes his hat and throws it into the stream. That is why Knurz catches the wind with a small trap.

Lila understands his problem. She does not want to frighten the troll with a spell. Instead, she takes a yellow ribbon out of her pocket. She ties the ribbon to the hat and to a button on the coat. Now the hat can fly, but it always stays with Knurz.

Knurz runs around the rock and jumps high into the air. The hat stays on his head. For the first time that day, the troll smiles. He gives Lila the blue bottle. Together they pull on the cork. But the cork is very tight, and both of them fall onto the soft forest floor.

Lila opens her spell book. She finds a spell for heavy doors and large jars. She raises her little wooden wand and says the words slowly. At first only a flower grows on the bottle. Then the cork makes a loud pop and flies high into an oak tree.

The north wind comes out of the bottle like a white ribbon. It turns around Lila and Knurz, lifts leaves from the ground and shakes the trees. Knurz holds his hat tightly, but the yellow ribbon is strong. The wind sees the ribbon and understands. From now on he leaves the red hat alone.

Lila sits on her broom, and Knurz sits behind her. The north wind carries them both over the forest. Below them, the sails of the windmill turn again. The washing dances on the lines, and the people in the village wave. Even the clouds finally move on.

In the evening Lila takes the troll back to his rock. Knurz gives her the blue bottle. It is empty now, but sometimes it sings very quietly. Lila puts it on the windowsill at home. When the north wind passes by, the bottle whistles, the broom knocks on the door, and Lila knows: it is time to fly.

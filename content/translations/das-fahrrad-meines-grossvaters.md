---
title: My Grandfather's Bicycle
---

In my grandparents' cellar there is an old bicycle that nobody has used for almost thirty years. It is dark green, has a leather saddle and weighs about as much as a small wardrobe. My grandfather rode it to work every day, in rain just as in snow.

When he retired, the bicycle was pushed into the furthest corner and forgotten there. My grandmother wanted to throw it out several times, but he refused every time. He always claimed that the bicycle would last longer than he would.

Last summer I brought it out again. The tyres were perished, the chain was completely rusted, and the paint was flaking off in many places. An expert would probably have advised me to take the bicycle to the scrapyard. All the same, I decided to repair it.

I worked in the garage for three weekends. Spare parts for a model from the sixties are of course not something you find at the DIY store. I ordered most of them online from collectors who restore such bicycles out of pure passion. If I had known beforehand how much work it would be, I probably would not have started at all.

By now the bicycle runs again. It is heavy, it has only three gears, and on the hill behind our village I have to get off and push. My friends laugh about it, although secretly they find it quite interesting.

But when I ride it through the fields on a warm evening, I understand why my grandfather never wanted to give it up.

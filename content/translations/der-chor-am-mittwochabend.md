---
title: The Choir on Wednesday Evening
---

I have been singing in a choir for six months. It all started with a note on the notice board at the supermarket saying that a choir was looking for new voices and that no previous experience was needed. I photographed the note and then did nothing about it for three weeks.

When I finally went to the first rehearsal, I was rather nervous. About twenty-five people were sitting in a semicircle in the parish hall, most of them a good deal older than me. The choir director, a friendly man with a grey beard, asked me whether I sang soprano or alto. I had to admit that I had no idea what the difference was.

Luckily nobody laughed. A woman in the second row made room for me next to her and said I should simply sing along and listen to her voice. In that first hour I listened more than I sang. All the same, I came back the following week.

By now I know that singing is far more tiring than it looks. We practise breathing, posture and pronunciation before we even start on the first song. When a piece will not come together, we repeat the same eight bars until everyone knows them in their sleep. Sometimes on a Wednesday after work I think I would rather stay at home, but I go along anyway.

In December we had our first performance in the little church on the market square. I was so frightened that I could hardly eat anything that afternoon. But once we were standing at the front and the first notes were hanging in the room, the nervousness was suddenly gone. My parents sat in the third row and claimed they had been able to pick my voice out clearly, which of course cannot be true.

What I like best are the moments just before the end of a rehearsal, when we get a song all the way through without a single mistake for the first time. For a few seconds the hall is then completely silent. If anyone had told me a year ago that I would one day sing every Wednesday evening of my own free will, I would not have believed them.

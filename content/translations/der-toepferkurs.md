---
title: The Pottery Class
---

For several months, Nina had wanted to learn something new after work. She sits at a computer for a long time every day and was therefore looking for a hobby where she could use her hands. She found a pottery class in the adult education centre's programme. The class takes place on six Thursday evenings in an old workshop.

On the first evening, Nina arrived ten minutes early. There were long tables, lots of shelves and two large potter's wheels in the workshop. Bowls, cups and small clay figures stood on the shelves. Some were still grey, while others already had shiny colours.

The course instructor was called David and wore an apron with lots of white marks. First he showed the eight participants how to prepare the clay. You have to press it firmly so that no air remains inside. Otherwise, the piece can break later in the hot kiln.

Nina wanted to make a cup straight away, but David recommended a simple bowl. She sat down at the potter's wheel and placed the clay exactly in the centre. When the wheel began to turn, she pressed much too hard. The clay became flat and finally flew onto the floor.

Nina was disappointed, but the woman beside her gave a friendly laugh. Her name was Marta, and it was her first time too. Together they collected the clay and tried again. On her second attempt, Nina moved her hands more slowly and produced a small round shape.

Over the next few weeks, the group learnt how to attach handles and draw patterns in the soft clay. Nina made two more bowls and a small cup. Not everything turned out the way she had planned. One cup stood crooked, but Marta thought that this one was especially beautiful.

After every evening, everyone had to clean their work area. The damp clay stuck to their hands, the table and sometimes even their hair. While they washed out sponges and sorted tools, the participants talked about their week. Nina realised that she liked these quiet conversations just as much as the pottery itself.

Before the final evening, David had fired all the pieces in the kiln. Now the participants were allowed to choose colours and paint their work. Nina chose a dark blue and painted a pale circle inside. Afterwards, the bowl had to go into the kiln a second time.

A week later, everyone could collect their finished things. Nina's first bowl was not completely round, and there was a small dent in the rim. Even so, the surface felt smooth and firm. Nina was proud because she had made the object with her own hands.

The bowl now stands on her mother's kitchen table and is filled with apples. Nina has already signed up for the next class, and Marta is coming back as well. Next time, Nina wants to make a large vase. David advised her to practise a few more small bowls first.

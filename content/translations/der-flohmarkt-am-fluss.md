---
title: The Flea Market by the River
---

Ever since we moved in, a shelf full of boxes we never unpacked has been standing in the cellar of our building. My friend Ida suggested selling the things at the flea market by the river instead of letting them stand there for another three years. You have to book a table in advance, and because a lot of people have the same idea, you have to do it early. We got our spot at the end of April, for a Sunday in June.

At six in the morning it was still cool, and mist lay over the water. Next to us an elderly couple were setting up their stall; they sell somewhere every weekend. The man explained to us straight away that you have to put the nice things at the front, because nobody walks round a table. We rearranged everything all over again.

The first customers came before we had finished. Mostly they are dealers who go quickly along the rows, hardly look at anything and find almost everything too expensive. Only around nine did the families with prams and coffee cups arrive. From then on we spent two hours talking more than selling.

The oddest things went: a typewriter with no ribbon, three saucepans and a box full of cassettes that a boy took away for his father. The books, on the other hand, the ones I was attached to, nobody wanted. By the end of the day we had ninety-six euros in the tin, and I had bought a lamp for fourteen euros from the stall next to ours. Ida says that on flea markets this is a law of nature.

In the evening we took what was left to the charity shop in two boxes. The shelf in the cellar is empty now, and the takings from twelve hours of work are roughly enough for dinner for two. Even so, we have already booked a table for next year.

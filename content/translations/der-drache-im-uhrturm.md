---
title: The Dragon in the Clock Tower
---

In the village of Lindenfels, the large clock on the market square showed exactly the right time every day. One morning, however, both hands stopped at seven o'clock. The clockmaker, Mr Winter, had injured his hand the day before and could not climb the many stairs in the tower. His daughter Lina therefore took the toolbox, although she had never repaired the clock on her own.

At the top of the tower, it was dark, warm and dusty. Lina first heard a quiet scratching sound, and then noticed a smell like burnt wood. Behind the largest cog sat a small green dragon whose tail was caught between two ropes. He was hardly bigger than a dog, but his copper-coloured wings filled almost the whole room.

Lina wanted to run straight back downstairs, but the dragon looked at her with tired eyes. Beside him lay a piece of cheese, three apples and half a loaf of bread that had disappeared from different kitchens in the village. Lina put her breakfast on the floor and waited until the animal became calm. Then she carefully cut through the rope and freed his tail.

The dragon was called Funke and could speak in a very quiet voice. During the night, a storm had separated him from his mother, and the light in the clock tower had shown him the way. He only wanted to rest for a few hours because his left wing was injured. When he landed, however, he had struck the ropes, and that had stopped several wheels from moving.

Lina climbed down and told her father what she had found. Soon the mayor knew about it too, and many excited neighbours gathered outside the clockmaker's house. Some wanted to close the tower, while others wanted to drive the dragon away with long poles. Lina explained to them that Funke was frightened and could not escape without a healthy wing.

The spring festival was due to begin the next day, so the clock had to run again quickly. Mr Winter gave Lina an ointment for Funke's wing and showed her how to make a firm bandage. The baker brought bread, and the gardener brought a bowl of water. In this way, the young dragon slowly realised that not all people were dangerous.

In the afternoon, Lina and her father examined the broken clock. A small metal pin was bent, and without it the most important cog could not turn. There was no suitable replacement in the village, but Funke had an idea. He heated the pin with a tiny burst of fire while Lina straightened the soft metal with a pair of pliers.

They then put all the parts back together and moved the hands slowly forwards. The clock began to tick, but no one knew whether the heavy bell would work as well. Funke was startled by every sound and hid behind Lina. She promised him that she would open the large balcony door before the first strike of the bell.

On the morning of the festival, many people were already standing in the market square. When the clock showed twelve, the bell rang loudly, and at the same moment Funke flew out of the tower. His injured wing was still weak, so at first he dropped a little lower. Lina called out encouragement, and the dragon rose above the roofs until he landed safely on the fountain.

The people stepped back, but no one reached for a pole or a stone. Funke waited until evening, and then a much larger dragon with copper-coloured wings appeared in the sky. His mother had heard the bell and found the village. Before the two flew away, she briefly touched Lina's hand with her nose.

Since that day, a small copper figure has hung above the entrance to the clock tower. It shows a dragon holding a cog in his paws. Lina later became a clockmaker herself, and every year at the spring festival she tells the story of her first important job. Sometimes she then sees two shining points above the mountains and wonders whether Funke is still listening for the sound of the bell.

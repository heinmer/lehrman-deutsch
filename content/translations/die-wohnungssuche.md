---
title: Looking for a Flat
---

When my flatmate got married in February and moved out, I had to find a place of my own. I had thought it would take four weeks. In the end it took five months, and in that time I learnt more about forms than about rooms.

Almost everything goes through two or three big websites nowadays. You set up a search alert, and when a suitable flat appears you get a message on your phone. Anyone who only replies in the evening need not bother at all, because for an ordinary flat several hundred people get in touch within a few hours. At some point I prepared a short text that I only had to copy.

At the first viewing I stood on the staircase with forty other people. The agent let us into the flat in groups of ten, and you had perhaps three minutes to look at everything. I hardly looked out of the window, because the whole time I was working out what to say if she asked me something. She did not ask me anything.

Anyone who wants to get a flat in Germany needs a whole folder: the last three months of payslips, a credit report from the Schufa, an identity card and a letter from the previous landlord confirming that you always paid on time. At first I found that unpleasant. Later I understood that without those papers nobody is taken seriously, and I sent the lot as a single file.

The flat I have now was never on the internet. A colleague told me that somebody on the third floor of her building was moving to Leipzig, and recommended me to the management company. Two days later I had the contract, without a viewing among forty strangers. It is fifty-two square metres, the bathroom is from 1978, and the rent is higher than I really wanted to pay.

At the handover we went through the rooms with a form and wrote down every scratch, including the one in the kitchen floor that you only see when the sun is low. The deposit is three months' rent without bills, and I will not get it back until I move out one day. Ever since, I have understood why people here move so rarely: the problem is not the flat, but the way to it.

---
title: The Map That Moved the Forest
---

Mara had worked in the royal cartographer's workshop for two years. Normally, she copied rivers, roads and borders from old sheets onto new paper. One morning, however, a map lay on her desk that had not been there the evening before. It showed only a thin green line that was moving slowly.

At the same time, the city was waiting in vain for a caravan from the north. It was supposed to bring salt, wool and important medicinal herbs, but had been missing for three days. Riders had followed its tracks to the edge of the Dark Forest, but there they suddenly ended. During the night, the forest had wandered several kilometres to the east and swallowed the old trade road.

Mara's master thought the green map was a bad joke. He explained that a real map should show only what lay in a fixed place. Mara noticed, however, that the line trembled whenever someone spoke the name of the forest. When small signs like wagon wheels appeared at the edge of the paper, she secretly took the map with her.

The next morning, Mara set off together with Jorin, the young forest ranger. As soon as they reached the first trees, the path behind them disappeared beneath ferns and roots. The trunks stood so close together that no sunlight reached the ground. Only the green line on the map remained bright and showed them a narrow direction.

On the way, they found a torn blanket, an empty barrel and finally deep wagon tracks. Yet the tracks led in a circle, although Mara was certain that they had walked straight ahead. Jorin wanted to turn back before they lost the exit as well. At that moment, a single silver tree appeared on the map.

They followed the new sign and came to a clearing where the missing caravan stood. The traders were unhurt, but their animals were tired and their supplies almost gone. Whenever they cut a path, other trees moved closer together and closed the gap again. The leader of the caravan claimed that the forest wanted to punish them for no reason.

Beside one wagon, Mara discovered several freshly felled young trees. The leader admitted that his people had needed wood for a broken axle. Soon afterwards, a tall figure wearing a cloak of leaves appeared between the trunks. The figure said that the forest would hold the caravan until what had been taken from it was replaced.

The traders had no saplings and could not bring the felled trees back to life. Mara looked at her map and noticed seven small green dots beside a stream. She led the group to that place, where young shoots were growing from the damp earth. Together, they carefully dug up the plants and carried them back to the clearing.

The figure demanded not only new trees, but also a place where their roots could grow. The traders therefore removed stones and rubbish from a damaged stretch of riverbank. They planted the shoots, gave them water and then repaired the wagon axle with boards from an old barrel. When the last sapling was standing, a broad path opened between the trees.

The caravan reached the city the following evening. Mara's master was relieved, but he immediately wanted to draw a permanent road through the forest on a new map. Mara explained to him that the path had appeared only because the traders had repaired the harm they had done. A line without that story would make future travellers believe that the forest always had to obey them.

The king summoned Mara and asked for the mysterious map. She gave it to him, but by then the paper was almost empty again. Only seven small trees could be seen on it, with a short green path running beneath them. The king understood that the map showed not a possession, but a relationship that changed with every action.

Mara was later allowed to open her own workshop. She continued to mark mountains, bridges and villages on her maps, but she always left an empty margin beside the Dark Forest. Travellers also received a bag of seeds from her and the advice to use only fallen wood. Sometimes a new green line would then appear overnight, promising them a safe path for a while.

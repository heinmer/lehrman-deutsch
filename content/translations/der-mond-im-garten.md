---
title: The Moon in the Garden
---

Nora lives with her grandmother in a small house at the edge of the village. Behind the house is a large garden. Apples, beans and many yellow flowers grow there. Nora's red cat Mino likes to sleep under the old apple tree. During the day the garden is quiet, but at night it sounds very different.

One evening Nora is still watering the flowers. The sun is already gone, and the first stars are in the sky. Suddenly Nora sees a silver light under a bush. Mino sees it too. He runs to the bush and then stands completely still.

Nora pushes the branches aside. A round ball is lying in the grass. It is as large as a pumpkin and glows with a soft white light. Nora touches it carefully. The ball is warm, and a small face opens in its light. Two bright eyes look at Nora.

“Please do not be afraid,” says the ball. “I am the moon.” Nora immediately looks up at the sky. There is only a dark circle among the stars. The little moon tells her about a strong wind. The wind comes from the mountains and throws him out of his path.

Nora asks how he can get back up there. The moon points to the high hill behind the village. An old white stone stands on the hill. At exactly midnight, the light of the stars touches this stone. From there the moon can jump back into the sky.

Nora gets her small wooden cart. She puts a soft blanket inside and sets the moon on it. Mino jumps in beside him. Then Nora pulls the cart through the garden gate. Grandma sees the silver light from the window. She gives Nora a lantern and a warm scarf for the journey.

The path to the hill leads through a dark field. The moon grows heavier and heavier because midnight is near. Nora pulls with both hands. Mino walks in front of the cart and shows the way. Sometimes a wheel gets stuck in the mud. Then even the little moon pushes with his short arms.

Soon they reach a stream. The old wooden bridge is very wet after the rain. Nora walks slowly, but the cart slides to the side. Mino jumps to the other end and holds the scarf tightly in his teeth. This keeps the cart on the bridge, and everyone reaches the other bank safely.

The white stone is waiting at the top of the hill. But a large cloud is in front of the stars. Nora, Mino and the moon sit down on the ground. They wait and listen to the wind in the trees. One minute before midnight, the wind pulls the cloud away. Many stars shine on the stone.

The moon rolls out of the cart and stands in the starlight. At first he is still small. Then he becomes larger and lighter. He floats above Nora and Mino and slowly rises into the sky. There he fills the dark circle with bright light again. The whole village now lies under his silver glow.

Nora and Mino go home. The empty cart is very light now. Grandma is waiting at the garden gate and smiles. That night Nora sleeps by the open window. The moon shines directly onto her bed. A small silver stone lies on the windowsill, and Nora knows exactly who leaves it there.

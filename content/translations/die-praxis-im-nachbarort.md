---
title: The Practice in the Next Village
---

Since the thirtieth of June the GP surgery in Wehrbach has been closed. The sign beside the door has been unscrewed, the patient records have been archived, and a note with two telephone numbers in the next village is stuck to the window. Doctor Reinhardt practised in this building for thirty-six years and latterly looked after some eleven hundred patients. Despite three advertisements he did not find a successor.

That a country practice finds no buyer has less to do with the place than with the profession. Whoever sets up takes on premises, equipment, staff and a financial risk that does not disappear for years. Most young doctors opt instead for a salaried post, often in a medical centre in the city, where regular working hours are taken for granted. It is hard to hold that against them.

For those left behind it is a question of distance. It is eleven kilometres to the nearest surgery, the bus runs twice a day, and anyone without a car sets aside half a morning for a prescription. The elderly are hit hardest, which is to say precisely those who would need to go most often.

Politicians are responding with bursaries, with quotas for university places and with grants for taking over a practice. Whether such instruments work will only become clear in ten years, since a medical degree takes as long as it takes. In Wehrbach the parish council is meanwhile discussing a different solution: a branch surgery staffed two days a week. It would no longer be a practice, but it would be something.

---
title: The Locksmith
---

On Tuesday evening I took the rubbish down. The flat door fell shut behind me, and the key was still lying on the kitchen table. There I stood on the staircase, in my socks, with an empty bin in my hand.

My neighbour let me in and lent me her phone. On the internet you find dozens of locksmiths, and all of them promise to be there in twenty minutes. I dialled the first number without asking about the price. That was my mistake.

The man arrived an hour later. He looked at the door, pushed a thin plastic card into the gap and pressed against it briefly; half a minute later the door was open. Then he wrote out a bill for two hundred and forty euros: call-out, evening surcharge, opening. When I said that was too much, he shrugged and said I was welcome to call the police.

I paid all the same, because I was tired and wanted to go to bed. The next day I rang the consumer advice centre and learnt that a price like that for a door that has simply fallen shut is not normal. Ever since, my neighbour has a spare key of mine, and I have hers.

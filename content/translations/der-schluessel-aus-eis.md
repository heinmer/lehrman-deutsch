---
title: The Key Made of Ice
---

During the winter holidays, Lea visits her aunt in a small town at the foot of the mountains. Behind the house begins an old park with a large pond. The pond has been frozen solid for a week. Lea is allowed to skate there, but she must always stay near the bank. Every afternoon she skates her rounds alone on the ice.

One day, shortly before sunset, Lea sees a blue light beneath her feet. At first she thinks that a lamp is lying there. Then she kneels down and discovers a long key in the clear ice. It looks like glass, but a small snowflake moves inside its handle. Lea carefully taps the thin spot with a branch.

Suddenly a white fox jumps out of a snowdrift beside her. His paws are blue, and his fur sparkles like fresh snow. The fox speaks in a quiet voice and asks Lea for help. The key belongs to the keeper of winter. A storm blew it across the whole park that morning.

Lea loosens the ice with some warm tea from her bottle. Then she takes the key and wraps it in her woollen scarf. The fox immediately runs towards the forest. Lea follows him although it is quickly growing dark. She knows that her aunt will soon be waiting for her, but the key must not remain in the park until morning.

The path leads between tall fir trees and becomes steeper and steeper. At a crossroads, all the signs are covered with snow. The fox finds the right path by a special smell. Later, a fallen tree lies across the way. Lea crawls under the branches and holds the cold key tightly against her chest.

Shortly before midnight, they reach a very old oak tree. In its trunk is a narrow door that Lea has never seen before. The ice key fits perfectly into the blue lock. Behind the door lies a bright garden beneath a dome of ice. White flowers grow there, and small clouds sleep on the ground.

In the middle of the garden, the keeper of winter sits on a stone chair. He is an old man with a coat made of snow. Since the storm, he has not been able to close the great clock of the seasons because the key is missing. Without this clock, winter remains in the valley for too long. Then the first spring flowers cannot grow.

Lea puts the key into the clock and turns it together with the keeper of winter. At first nothing moves. The white fox places both front paws on the wheel, and suddenly it begins to move slowly. Water drips from the icicles beneath the dome. One of the little clouds wakes up and flies out through an open window.

The keeper of winter thanks Lea and gives her a silver snowflake. Afterwards the fox takes her back to the pond along a shorter path. The ice is still solid, but at the edge Lea can already hear quiet water. Her aunt is waiting in front of the park with a lantern. Lea tells her the whole story on the way home.

Her aunt believes that Lea only saw a particularly beautiful fox in the forest. At home Lea hangs the silver snowflake above her bed. The next morning warm sunlight shines through the window, and the first snow on the roof begins to melt. Lea discovers three small blue paw prints on the windowsill. Then she knows that the keeper of winter is watching over his clock again.
